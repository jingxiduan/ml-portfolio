# Is the cross-industry difference provable, and separable?

Two claims are frequently conflated in this project, and they have different status. They
are therefore tested separately.

| claim | what it asserts | status |
|---|---|---|
| **C1 · separability** | the three trained policies produce motion statistics that differ measurably, and the policy that produced a clip can be identified above chance | **supported**, see section 3 |
| **C2 · ecological validity** | that difference corresponds to a difference that actually exists between the corresponding real industries | **partially supported**, see section 2; fails for finance versus real-estate |

A third claim is explicitly rejected:

| claim | status |
|---|---|
| **C3 · discovery** | OPSD discovered the industry difference | **rejected**. The difference is prescribed by the measured bands. OPSD realises a prescribed band without overshoot; it does not infer the band. |

---

## 1. Test design

Two experiments, both reported with effect sizes, confidence intervals and a permutation or
binomial null rather than with point estimates alone.

**Q1, the ceiling.** Per-clip motion statistics measured on the real public footage
(`outputs/industry_bands_full.json`; finance n = 25, real-estate n = 16, short-video n = 16).
If the footage itself does not separate two industries, no policy trained on those bands can
be expected to separate them, and any model-side separation for that pair must be
attributed to the policy rather than to the industry.

**Q2, the policies.** Motion generated by `base` and the three personas on the **same**
40 held-out AISHELL-1 evaluation clips, with identical audio, seed and decoding settings, so
the comparison is paired and clip-level variance cancels. Per-clip low-band amplitude
(0.3 to 3 Hz) and high-band jitter (6 to 12.5 Hz) in degrees, averaged over pitch, yaw and
roll.

Statistics: Mann-Whitney U with the probability of superiority, AUC, and Cliff's delta;
bootstrap 95 % confidence interval of the median difference; Kruskal-Wallis across the three
groups; leave-one-out nearest-centroid classification with a 5000-fold permutation null; and
for the paired model data a Wilcoxon signed-rank test, a paired t-test, and a concordance
test of the ordering prescribed by the measured bands against the 1/6 chance level.

Script: `scripts/eval_separation.py`, outputs `outputs/separation_real.json` and
`outputs/separation_model.json`.

---

## 2. Q1 · is the difference present in the real footage?

| pair | median amplitude | ratio | AUC | Cliff's δ | Mann-Whitney p | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| finance vs real-estate | 1.384 vs 1.257 deg | 1.10× | **0.632** | +0.26 | **0.16** | **not separable** |
| short-video vs real-estate | 3.039 vs 1.257 deg | 2.42× | 1.000 | +1.00 | 1.5e-06 | separable |
| short-video vs finance | 3.039 vs 1.384 deg | 2.20× | 1.000 | +1.00 | 9.7e-08 | separable |

Three-way leave-one-out classification on log amplitude: accuracy **0.702** against a chance
level of 0.333, permutation p = 2e-4. The confusion matrix shows where that accuracy comes
from:

| true \ predicted | finance | real-estate | short-video |
|---|---|---|---|
| finance | **16** | 9 | 0 |
| real-estate | 8 | **8** | 0 |
| short-video | 0 | 0 | **16** |

Short-video is classified perfectly. Finance and real-estate are confused at close to chance,
8 of 25 finance clips and 8 of 16 real-estate clips being assigned to the other class.

**Two caveats that limit even the separable pairs.**

1. **The short-video separation is partly definitional.** Real-estate and short-video are the
   middle and upper amplitude quantiles of the same corpus, CelebV-HQ. Selecting groups by
   measured amplitude and then showing that they differ in measured amplitude is circular.
   What is established is that a high-amplitude speaking style exists and is well separated
   from a formal broadcast style, not that the corpus contains a real-estate industry.
2. **Finance and real-estate do not differ in this data.** Their interquartile ranges overlap
   almost completely, 1.18 to 1.57 deg against 1.03 to 1.50 deg, and the bootstrap interval
   for the median difference, minus 0.179 to plus 0.474 deg, contains zero. Any downstream
   separation of these two personas is therefore produced by the training target, not
   supported by the reference data.

## 3. Q2 · are the trained personas separable?

Amplitude, 40 paired clips per variant:

| pair | median amplitude | ratio | AUC | Cliff's δ | Mann-Whitney p | paired t p | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| finance vs real-estate | 1.213 vs 1.114 deg | 1.09× | 0.816 | +0.63 | 1.2e-06 | 7.6e-08 | separable |
| short-video vs finance | 2.597 vs 1.213 deg | 2.14× | 1.000 | +1.00 | 1.4e-14 | 6.4e-39 | separable |
| short-video vs real-estate | 2.597 vs 1.114 deg | 2.33× | 1.000 | +1.00 | 1.4e-14 | 2.6e-44 | separable |
| finance vs base | 1.213 vs 0.924 deg | 1.31× | 0.974 | +0.95 | 3.1e-13 | 6.1e-18 | separable |
| real-estate vs base | 1.114 vs 0.924 deg | 1.21× | 0.936 | +0.87 | 2.1e-11 | 1.2e-10 | separable |

Kruskal-Wallis across the four variants: p = 1.2e-28.

**Classification.** Four-way leave-one-out, chance 0.25:

| feature set | accuracy | permutation p |
|---|---|---|
| log amplitude only | **0.831** | 2e-4 |
| log amplitude and log jitter | 0.600 | 2e-4 |

| true \ predicted, amplitude only | base | finance | real-estate | short-video |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| base | **36** | 2 | 2 | 0 |
| finance | 0 | **31** | 9 | 0 |
| real-estate | 3 | 11 | **26** | 0 |
| short-video | 0 | 0 | 0 | **40** |

**Ordering.** The ordering prescribed by the measured bands,
short-video > finance > real-estate, is reproduced in **33 of 40** clips, a fraction of
0.825 against a chance level of 0.167, binomial p = 1.1e-19.

**Conclusion on C1.** The three personas are separable well above chance, the effect sizes
are large, and the prescribed ordering is reproduced on most held-out clips. Claim C1 is
supported.

---

## 4. Why the model separates better than the real footage, and why that is not evidence

For finance versus real-estate the policies separate at AUC 0.816 while the real footage
separates at AUC 0.632, which is not significant. The policies are therefore *more*
distinguishable than the populations they were calibrated on. The reason is visible in the
dispersion:

| condition | within-condition coefficient of variation |
|---|---|
| real footage, finance | 0.226 |
| real footage, real-estate | 0.212 |
| real footage, short-video | 0.153 |
| model, finance | 0.066 |
| model, real-estate | 0.068 |
| model, short-video | 0.060 |

The generated motion is roughly two to three times more self-consistent than real human
motion, because the reward drives every clip towards the same band centre regardless of
speaker, prosody or content. A smaller within-condition variance mechanically inflates every
separability statistic. **Model-side separability must therefore never be read as evidence
that the underlying industry difference exists.** In the finance versus real-estate case the
model manufactures a distinction that the reference data does not support.

---

## 5. The separation is one-dimensional

Adding jitter as a second feature *reduces* leave-one-out accuracy from 0.831 to 0.600.
Jitter carries no usable class information because it is not delivered as prescribed: the
short-video jitter target is 0.349 deg and the policy reaches 0.151 deg, a ratio of 0.43×
against the amplitude ratio of 0.84×. The personas therefore differ along one axis, the
amplitude of head motion, which is by the argument in `docs/NATURALNESS_SPEC.md` the single
quantity most weakly related to perceived naturalness.

A claim of distinct industry *styles* requires at least two realised behavioural axes.

---

## 6. Verdict

| question | answer |
|---|---|
| Can OPSD produce three policies that are measurably different and identifiable above chance? | **Yes.** Four-way identification accuracy 0.831 against chance 0.25, permutation p = 2e-4; prescribed ordering reproduced in 33 of 40 clips, p = 1.1e-19. |
| Does that prove the industries differ? | **No.** The difference is prescribed by the measured bands. OPSD realises the prescription without overshoot, 0.84 to 0.90× target, which is the method's contribution. |
| Is the prescribed difference itself real? | **Only for short-video versus the formal style.** Finance versus real-estate is not separable in the reference footage, AUC 0.632, p = 0.16. |
| Is the difference a style, or a scalar? | **A scalar.** Adding a second prescribed axis, jitter, reduces identification accuracy to 0.600. |

---

## 7. What would upgrade the claim

1. **Sources that actually differ.** Replace the two CelebV-HQ amplitude quantiles with
   footage that carries an independent industry signal, for example property-tour narration
   against news broadcasting, and re-measure. Until then only the energetic versus formal
   contrast is supported.
2. **Realise a second axis.** Silence-segment behaviour, onset phase locking and blink rate
   are the candidates already specified in `docs/NATURALNESS_SPEC.md`. A persona must be
   separated from the others by at least two independently measured statistics before the
   word style is defensible.
3. **Report the ceiling alongside the model.** Every separability figure should be presented
   next to the corresponding real-footage figure, as in sections 2 and 3, so that a model
   separation exceeding the human ceiling is visible rather than being read as an improvement.
4. **Move the test to video space and to human judgement.** The statistics above are computed
   on motion coefficients produced by the model itself. The E1 and E2 blind tests of
   `docs/NATURALNESS_SPEC.md` remain the only unbypassable criterion and have not been run.

## 8. Reproduce

```bash
python scripts/eval_separation.py --kind real                       # CPU, per-clip bands
python scripts/eval_separation.py --kind model --n-clips 40 --gpu 0 # GPU, held-out speakers
```
